**Paper Title**: StateShift: A Confirmatory Evaluation of Reasoning-Context Continuation and Forced Restart in Mathematical Language Models  
**Track**: Main Conference Track (Research Track)  
**Primary Subject Area**: Large Language Models / Reasoning & Planning  
**Secondary Subject Area**: Evaluation & Benchmark Methodology / Reproducibility  

## Abstract
Standard evaluations of reinforcement-learning post-training in large language models emphasize aggregate single-turn benchmark accuracy (e.g., Pass@1), potentially obscuring behavioral dynamics conditional on intermediate reasoning state. We introduce StateShift, a controlled diagnostic evaluation framework for evaluating reasoning recovery under matched counterfactual conditions. In Study 1, evaluating the post-training trajectory of the DeepScaleR-7B lineage ($N=454$ decontaminated competition problems, $29,056$ row-level outcome records), we isolate the State-Matched Interaction Contrast ($\Gamma_t$), which differences out baseline problem difficulty and forward proficiency gains. Between the un-tuned base model ($t=0$) and the terminal checkpoint ($t=256$), we observe a statistically significant interaction gain of $\Gamma_{256} = +11.76\text{ percentage points}$ ($95\%$ bootstrap CI $[+9.61, +13.96]\text{ pp}$, cluster-robust $p < 10^{-20}$), while the raw problem-level correlation between clean gains and recovery gains is modest ($r = 0.0794, p = 0.091$). In Study 2, an unpreregistered discovery pilot on physical NVIDIA RTX 4090 hardware ($400$ neural generations with Qwen2.5-Math-7B-Instruct, $N=100$) suggested an apparent $+6.00\text{ pp}$ empirical advantage for state-preserving continuation over forced restart ($73.0\%$ vs $67.0\%$, odds ratio $4:1$, exact two-sided $p = 0.1094$). Exact discrete binomial power modeling revealed that $N=100$ achieved only $36.19\%$ unconditional power, establishing $N=231$ as the preregistered minimum sample size required for $80\%$ power. In Study 3, we executed the frozen, preregistered confirmatory trial across $231$ held-out MATH-500 problems ($924$ authentic RTX 4090 neural generations across four conditions: A, D, E, F). The primary preregistered contrast refutes the pilot's point estimate: state-preserving continuation achieves $67.97\%$ ($157/231$) versus $68.40\%$ ($158/231$) for forced restart, yielding $\hat{\tau}_{F-D} = -0.0043 \quad (-0.43\%)$ ($95\%$ paired Wald CI $[-4.50\%, +3.64\%]$, Newcombe score CI $[-4.51\%, +3.65\%]$, exact two-sided McNemar $p = 1.000000$), strictly excluding the pilot's $+6.00\text{ pp}$ effect. Dual-evaluator parsing confirms zero discrepancies ($0/924$, $100\%$ invariance). Qualitative trace inspection of the $23$ discordant problems ($b=11$ continuation-only successes, $c=12$ restart-only successes) indicates that performance differences are shaped by multiple competing factors: while prefix retention can conserve token budget on lengthy derivations ($8/11$ cases in $b$ truncated under restart), it can also induce repetitive verification loops or anchor generation to flawed intermediate premises ($7/12$ truncations, $5/12$ derivation slips in $c$). Because token budget and prefix validity were not factorially manipulated, the experiment identifies the net effect of the bundled prompt intervention rather than isolated mechanistic components. These findings demonstrate that inference-time search architectures cannot assume that preserving partial reasoning context is uniformly superior to a clean restart.
